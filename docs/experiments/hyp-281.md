# Hypothesis #281: UCX gaps through rotated Blender FBX roundtrips at 14 km

**Verdict: REJECTED.** One pair breaks the 0.5 mm limit, in both repetitions: the 3 mm gap at 37° and
(-1170,-14200,0) m reads back as **2.4401 mm**, an error of **0.5599 mm** against a limit of 0.5 mm.
The class is still in_band. All other mandatory checks pass. `delivery_passed=false`; the geometry is
synthetic stress geometry, not data from a real object.

## Claim (issue #281)
The unchanged path mesh dump -> Blender 5.1.2 `export_mesh_blender.py` -> FBX -> fresh Blender import keeps
nominal 3/4/6 mm parallel box gaps within 0.5 mm of nominal and inside the 2-10 mm band. This holds after
rotation by 0/37/89° and translation up to (-1170,-14200,0) m, with no recentering.

## Findings
1. **The cause is float32 vertex storage, not FBX.** At every pair, the imported gap equals the gap computed
   on the source vertices rounded to float32 in pure Python. The largest difference is 6e-16 m.
   - At |y| ≈ 14 200 m, one float32 step (ulp) is 2^-10 m ≈ 0.98 mm, so each coordinate can move by up to
     about 0.49 mm.
   - A rotated face is no longer axis-aligned, so its 4 corners round independently and the face tilts.
     With 37° rotation the 3 mm gap loses 0.56 mm.
   - Near the origin the error is at most 0.2 µm. At 100 m it is at most 3.3 µm.
2. Even at 14 km, the 3-6 mm gaps stay in_band, but the margin does not hold up:
   - The worst errors are 0.56 / 0.29 / 0.43 mm.
   - A nominal 2 mm gap at this magnitude would likely read back below_band.
   - The `BAND_TOL_M = 1e-5` comment ("2.000 mm reads back within 0.01 mm") holds only near the origin.
     That comment is outside this card; I did not change it.
3. The importer's `matrix_world` deviates from identity by at most 1.6e-7. The transform is rigid, so the
   gaps are not affected.

## Verdict
REJECTED: the imported gap error is 0.5599 mm against the 0.5 mm threshold. The failure is the same in both
native repetitions. Per the issue, there is no adoption and nothing goes to `src/`.
